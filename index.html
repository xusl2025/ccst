IIIThe group

Six turns, and everything they generate.

Name every quarter turn after the face it moves. That is the whole alphabet — a cube position is nothing but the product of the letters that produce it.

A prime reverses a turn; a two doubles it. Sequences read left to right, so U R F² is Up, then Right, then Front twice.

Every product of these six letters is the Rubik’s cube group. We will call it G.

G = ⟨U, D, L, R, F, B⟩

IIIThe group

A group is a set that closes.

Take every sequence of the six turns. The distinct positions you reach form the cube group.

I

Associativity

Bracketing is free: three turns group either way and still mean the same move.

(U · R) · F = U · (R · F)

II

Inverses

Every turn can be undone: U then U′ leaves the cube untouched.

U · U′ = e

III

Identity

The do-nothing move is an element too: the solved cube.

U4 = e  ·  a turn has order four

Nothing escapes the set. A scramble and a solution are the same object — so shortest becomes a question about distance.

IIIThe group

Forty-three quintillion.

The number is not a mystery. Eight corners and twelve edges, each with a position and an orientation — divided by the two constraints that make a cube legal.
